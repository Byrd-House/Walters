Every spring we get the same question: *when should I mulch?* Around Chapel Hill and Durham, the honest answer is mid-spring — once the soil has warmed and the last hard frost is behind us, usually April. Mulch too early and you trap cold around roots that are trying to wake up. Wait too long and you miss the window where it does the most good.

Here's how we think about it.

## Timing

We like to mulch after the spring cleanup is done — beds edged, weeds pulled, last year's leaves cleared. Putting fresh mulch over a messy bed just buries the problem for a few weeks. A second, lighter touch-up in early fall keeps things looking cared for through the holidays.

## How much

Two to three inches is the sweet spot. That's enough to hold moisture and keep weeds down, but not so much that water can't reach the roots. Piling mulch up against tree trunks — the "mulch volcano" you see everywhere — actually invites rot. Keep it pulled back a few inches from trunks and stems.

## The details that matter

- **Edge first.** A clean, hand-cut edge between bed and lawn is what makes mulch look finished instead of dumped.
- **Pick a color and stick with it.** Switching from brown to black to red year to year reads as an afterthought.
- **Don't skip the weeding.** Mulch slows weeds; it doesn't stop the ones already rooted underneath.
